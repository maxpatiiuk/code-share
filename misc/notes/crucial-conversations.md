# Crucial Conversations

## Summary of summary

In crucial conversation, pause, listen, and think.

Mistakes: assume the worst. treat story as facts (what made me think this? why would they act like this?)

1. Identify the right conversation (occurrence, pattern, relationship)
2. Separate facts from stories
3. Conversation:
   - Share good intent first. Unhelpful short-thinking: being right, blame. Helpful: build long-term trust - reduces friction, learn
   - Share your facts (facts are neutral). Less is more
   - Tell your story (interpretation). Why impactful, important
   - Thoughts? What happened?
4. "I don't mean ... I wanted to have a discussion about ..." if misunderstood. Avoid "but"

Restore safety in conversation:

1. Agree to agree. It sounds like we are going in circles. Lets take stock of what we hold in common
2. Ask Why. What happened? What is it about my proposal that doesn't work for you? Makes sense. Anything else?
3. Find the "And". Look for combined purpose. Look for higher-level, longer-term purpose
   - If we find a way for you to get X and for me to get Y, would that work for you?
4. Brainstorming - don't jump to problem solving. Learn the why and establish trust

If silent treatment: What are you thinking? You look sad - why? Let's see if I understood correctly. Is it something that I've done that has you upset?

If verbal violence: "you are right", "absolutely, in addition", "my experience is different from yours - can I share it?"

## Get unstack

Crucial conversation:

- Opposing opinions
- Strong emotions
- High stakes

silence --- verbal violence continuum.
Blood flow reduced - harder to have productive conversation. Workaround: ask yourself a thoughtful question - causes the blood to return to the brain

Content - 80%. A single instance of a problem; the action or its immediate consequences. In the moment.
Pattern - 15%. A recurring problem - a pattern of behavior
Relationship - 5%. How the problem affects your working relationship - trust is suffering

## Master my stories

- See & hear - the situation
- Tell a story - assumption, interpretation - too quick
- Feel - feel emotional about the story
- Act - act on the emotions. Escalating the spiral

Mistakes:

- Assume the worst when there aren't enough facts
- Treat stories as facts

**Start with the least escalatory version of the conversation**. Curious over furious

Fact: objective, observation, measurement

Stories: subjective judgments, conclusions

To separate facts from stories, "what did I see that made me think this?"

Stories:

- Victim - not my fault. What's my role?
- Villain - it's their fault. Why would a reasonable, rational, decent person do this?
- Helpless - powerless to do anything helpful. What can I do now?

## Start with heart

Unhelpful intent (Me):

- Be right
- Look good, save face
- Win
- Punish, blame
- Avoid conflict
- Be comfortable
- Control

Intent of dialogue (We):

- Find the truth
- Learn
- Find a win-win situation
- Produce long-term results
- Strengthen relationships

Intent is the first to deteriorate in crucial conversations

"What do my actions communicate about my intent?". Share the good intent first
"What do I really want" (for me, other person, relationship, organiztion)? Want a win win

## State my path

0. Statement of good intent
1. Share your facts (facts are neutral). Less is more
2. Tell your story (interpretation). Why impactful, important
3. Ask for others' paths.
   - **Thoughts?**
   - **What happened?**
   - How do you see it?
   - Can you help me understand your perspective?
   - What's your view of this?
   - Too soon for:
     - What will it take to fix this? How can we prevent it? How to solve it?

Avoid always/never/liar/loser

## Make it safe

Contrast to fix misunderstandings, if there is one:

- My intent is not to.... I do want to focus on
- I'sm not trying to suggest... What I am hoping we can solve
- My goal isn't to... I do want to discuss

Avoids "But".

## Learn to look

Silence:

- Understating true thoughts (sarcasm, sugarcoating)
- Using "some people" over I
- Changing the topic
- Withdrawing from conversations

Verbal violence:

- Exaggerating via absolutes
- Name-calling
- Monopolizing conversation
- Using directive questions
- Belittling ideas or others
- Threatening
- Workspace:
  - Pinging
  - Reopening discussions untimely

## Seek mutual purpose

Restore safety to the conversation:

1. Agree to agree
   - "I'm not the enemy". Empathy
   - It sounds like we are going in circles. Lets take stock of what we hold in common
2. Ask Why
   - Safety is created through a shared purpose. To find a mutual purpose, understand other person's purpose.
   - What happened?
   - What is it about my proposal that doesn't work for you?
   - Makes sense. Anything else?
3. Find the "And"
   - Look for combined purpose. Look for higher-level, longer-term purpose
   - If we find a way for you to get X and for me to get Y, would that work for you?
4. Brainstorming - don't jump to problem solving. Learn the why and establish trust

## Explore Other's Paths

If they aren't speaking up enough, try one of:

- Ask with intent
  - What are you thinking?
- Mirror to reflect what you are seeing
  - You look sad about this. May I ask why?
- Paraphrase to confirm understanding
  - Let's see if I understood correctly...
- Prime with questions
  - Is it something I've done that has you upset?

In response to verbal violence, pick one:

- Agree if you agree
  - You are right
- Build on top if incomplete
  - Look for agreement pieces, not magnify disagreements
  - "Absolutely. In addition, I noticed that..."
  - "I agree with your concerns. Fortunately, we already taken steps to address that."
- Compare when you differ
  - My experience is different from yours. Can I share it?

Ask for a pause if need to calm down the emotions
